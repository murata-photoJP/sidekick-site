Sidekick Lab/Sidekick Planner
In development

Sidekick Planner

A shot-planning tool for photographers that finds the
when and where your sky object and your landscape line up.

This is not a product page. Sidekick Planner is currently in development. There is no release yet, and availability, pricing, and how it will be distributed are not decided. What is written here is limited to what actually runs as of 31 August 2026, plus the scope planned for Version 1. Why it is being built is described in Story: "It Started with 'When and Where Should I Go?'"

What this software does

It is not software that produces the right answer for a photograph.

The sun sitting on the summit of Mt. Fuji. The moon sitting there. The Milky Way arriving where you wanted it. Taking photographs like those requires more than shooting technique. Without knowing when and where to go, nothing begins.

The position of a celestial object, the coordinates of a location, the terrain in between: once the date, time, and place are fixed, all of that can be calculated. Leave what can be calculated to the computer, and find the candidates a photographer can then judge for themselves. That is as far as Sidekick Planner tries to go.

What comes after — whether you actually go, whether you shift position once you are there, whether you call it off because of the weather, whether you abandon the planned photograph and shoot something else — is for the photographer to decide.

Current status (as of 31 August 2026)

So that things still under consideration are not read as product commitments, what actually runs today is listed separately from what is still to be decided.

Running in the validation prototype

  • Two-way search for sun/moon alignments: candidate locations, and the dates and times they occur
  • A "nightscape / sun and moon" genre (a core set of stars, constellations, and planets, plus the Milky Way)
  • Picking a point on the map and listing the resulting candidates
  • Terrain and building silhouettes toward the target, from elevation data and 3D city models
  • A composition preview for the chosen date, time, and location

Scope planned for Version 1

  • Delivered as an application for Windows PC
  • Two-way search — candidate locations and occurrence times — for the sun and moon
  • Milky Way limited to direction, altitude, tilt, and start/end of visibility
  • Targeted at locations in Japan, with times shown in Japan Standard Time
  • Calculation runs locally; no external AI APIs are used
  • Results exported as text/CSV, with sharing left to the operating system

Not yet decided

  • Availability, distribution model, and price
  • How data is distributed and updated
  • Detailed system requirements

Out of scope for Version 1

  • Web and smartphone versions (candidates for the future)
  • Milky Way arch compositions and precise composition search (Version 2 onwards)
  • Cloud-cover forecast map overlays (set aside on licensing and cost grounds)
  • In-app sharing features

What can be calculated, and what cannot

A new moon, little light pollution, a forecast of clear skies. On conditions alone, it is perfect. And even so, the Milky Way looks different every night, because the transparency of the air and the amount of water vapour differ.

The forecast is not wrong. The astronomical calculation is not wrong. It is simply that some things can be known by calculation and some can only be known on site.

So Sidekick Planner does not promise that "go here and you will get a good photograph." It calculates what can be known in advance and takes it as far as the point where a photographer can make a judgement. To see what lies beyond that, the photographer goes to the location.

Why I decided to build it

It started with a single Pearl Fuji shoot, more than ten years ago.
