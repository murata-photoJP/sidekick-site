Sidekick Planner

From the image you want, to where and when to shoot.
A shot-planning tool for photographers.

This page explains the thinking behind Sidekick Planner. What the current beta 1.00 can do, its screens and the download are on the product page, "Sidekick Planner Beta 1.00" (the beta's product UI is Japanese).
Why it is being built is described in Story: "It Started with 'When and Where Should I Go?'"

What this software does

It is not software that produces the right answer for a photograph.

The sun sitting on the summit of Mt. Fuji. The moon sitting there. Taking photographs like those requires more than shooting technique. Without knowing when and where to go, nothing begins.

The position of a celestial object, the coordinates of a location, the terrain in between: once the date, time, and place are fixed, all of that can be calculated. Leave what can be calculated to the computer, and find the candidates a photographer can then judge for themselves. That is as far as Sidekick Planner tries to go.

What comes after — whether you actually go, whether you shift position once you are there, whether you call it off because of the weather, whether you abandon the planned photograph and shoot something else — is for the photographer to decide.

What Sidekick Planner does

What beta 1.00 actually offers today. It may change with later updates.

  • Find candidate shooting points and the dates on which the sun lines up with a subject (mountains, towers, castles, temples and more)
  • Draw the terrain between the shooting point and the subject as a skyline, to check nothing in between blocks the view
  • From a chosen date and place, overlay the sun's track and the lens's field of view to check the composition
  • Turn a finished plan into a QR code and take it to a smartphone or another PC

Plans that place the moon on a subject (Pearl) are coming soon. The software currently targets Windows PC, for use in Japan.

What can be calculated, and what cannot

Sidekick Planner is not software that decides "go here and you are certain to get the shot" or "this composition is the right one". It calculates how the sun or the moon lines up with a subject such as Mt. Fuji, and finds the places and times where a photograph may be possible, as material for the photographer's own judgement.

Calculation and data can tell you where the sun or the moon will be and when, how it lines up with the subject, which candidate points to stand on and on which dates it works. Whether the terrain in between hides the subject, and how the atmosphere lifts the apparent position, are also included — computed from elevation data and a standard atmosphere.

Only the location can tell you that day's weather and visibility, the trees in front of you, buildings that are not in the map or the elevation data, construction work, access restrictions, and whether the spot is safe. None of that is in the calculation.

So Sidekick Planner does not say "go here and you will get a good photograph." It gets the calculable part out of the way in advance and takes it as far as the point where a photographer can make a judgement. To see what lies beyond that, the photographer goes to the location.
