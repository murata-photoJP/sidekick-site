Sidekick Planner

A shot-planning tool for photographers that finds the
when and where your sky object and your landscape line up.

This is not a product page. Sidekick Planner is currently in development, and there is nothing to use yet. Availability, pricing, and other details will be announced once decided.
Why it is being built is described in Story: "It Started with 'When and Where Should I Go?'"

What this software does

It is not software that produces the right answer for a photograph.

The sun sitting on the summit of Mt. Fuji. The moon sitting there. The Milky Way arriving where you wanted it. Taking photographs like those requires more than shooting technique. Without knowing when and where to go, nothing begins.

The position of a celestial object, the coordinates of a location, the terrain in between: once the date, time, and place are fixed, all of that can be calculated. Leave what can be calculated to the computer, and find the candidates a photographer can then judge for themselves. That is as far as Sidekick Planner tries to go.

What comes after — whether you actually go, whether you shift position once you are there, whether you call it off because of the weather, whether you abandon the planned photograph and shoot something else — is for the photographer to decide.

The main features being built

This is software in development, so the details may change.

  • Find candidate locations and dates from how the sun or moon lines up with a landscape
  • Check the direction and altitude of the Milky Way, and when it is visible
  • Plan a shoot while checking whether mountains or buildings will block the view
  • Check the composition from a chosen date, time, and location

Development is currently aimed at Windows PC, for use in Japan.

What can be calculated, and what cannot

A new moon, little light pollution, a forecast of clear skies. On conditions alone, it is perfect. And even so, the Milky Way looks different every night, because the transparency of the air and the amount of water vapour differ.

The forecast is not wrong. The astronomical calculation is not wrong. It is simply that some things can be known by calculation and some can only be known on site.

So Sidekick Planner does not promise that "go here and you will get a good photograph." It calculates what can be known in advance and takes it as far as the point where a photographer can make a judgement. To see what lies beyond that, the photographer goes to the location.

Why I decided to build it

It started with a single Pearl Fuji shoot, more than ten years ago.
